How it works, and tips

Setup, once

  1. Keep this file on both phones. Send it once by Bluetooth, Quick Share or a cable, or download it while you have internet.
  2. Open it in Chrome or Samsung Internet. It runs fully offline; the photo never leaves the phone except as the text you send.

Sending

  • Crop close. A face filling the frame comes out far clearer than a face in a crowd.
  • Pick anywhere from 1 to 99 SMS. Every extra SMS makes the picture bigger and sharper: 3 gives the idea, 10 to 20 looks good, 50 or more looks close to the photo.
  • The text uses only plain letters, digits and !#%()*+,-./:;=?, so each SMS holds its full 153 characters (160 for a single SMS). If your phone has an SMS “input mode” setting, keep it on Automatic or GSM; Unicode mode cuts each SMS to 70 characters.
  • Long texts are split into messages of a few SMS each. Some phones turn long texts into MMS, which needs mobile data; if yours does, pick fewer SMS per message. Many plans also cap SMS per day.

Receiving

  • Copy each message and paste it in the other tab. Order, spaces and line breaks don’t matter, and repeats are ignored.
  • The numbered boxes show which SMS have arrived. The picture appears from SMS 01 onwards and gets sharper with every SMS that follows in order.
  • If one goes missing, the boxes show which number to ask for again.

Under the hood

The photo is resized to roughly five pixels for every bit you can send (about 144 × 96 for 3 SMS and 768 × 512 for 99), split into wavelets (the same idea JPEG 2000 uses), and coded most-important-detail-first with SPIHT and an adaptive arithmetic coder. Every bit of the budget gets used. Each SMS carries its number and its own checksum, about 117 bytes of picture each.
